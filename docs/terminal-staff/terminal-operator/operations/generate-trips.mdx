Timeslots are templates. A **Trip Sync Job** is what turns them into the actual
**departure trips** that passengers get checked in to.

> **Trip Sync Jobs → New Sync Job → review the preview → Execute Job**

A sync job compares, for every date in a range, the **active timeslots** that apply
to that date against the **trips that already exist**, and then creates what is
missing and removes what no longer belongs.

:::note
You need permission to manage trip synchronizations. The screenshots below use
`https://ts-terminal.sindoferry.com.sg/`.
:::

## Before you start

- You are signed in to the Terminal Portal — see
  [Sign In to the Terminal Portal](/terminal-staff/sign-in).
- The [timeslots](/terminal-operator/create-timeslot) you want trips for exist and
  are **Active**.

:::info[Most of the time the system has already done this for you]
Alongside the jobs you run by hand, the system runs **its own sync once a day**. It
shows up in the list as **Requested By: System (Daily Auto-Sync)** with a remark
like _"Automated daily rolling sync from 29-Sep-2026 to 29-Oct-2026"_.

That automatic job covers a **rolling window 60 to 90 days ahead**, across **all
operators, all routes and all days** (public holidays included), and it executes
itself immediately — there is no preview to approve.

This is why trips usually **already exist** for dates a couple of months out, and
why a manual job for a near date often previews **0 to create**. Run one by hand
when you need trips **sooner than the automatic window reaches**, or when you have
just changed a timeslot and want the trips rebuilt **now**.
:::

## Step 1 — Open Trip Sync Jobs

In the sidebar, open **Management → Trip Sync Jobs**
(route: `/departure-trip-synchronizations`). The page is headed **Trip
Synchronizations** and lists previous jobs, one row each:

| Column | Shows |
| --- | --- |
| **Date Range** | The period the job covers, with the job's remark beneath it |
| **Operator** / **Route** | The job's scope — **All** when left blank |
| **Results** | **+N created** and **−N deleted** |
| **Status** | **Pending**, **Enqueued**, or **Completed** |
| **Requested By** | Who created it, and when |

**Show Filters** narrows the list; **New Sync Job** (top right) starts one.

![Trip Synchronizations list](/img/terminal-staff/generate-departure-trips/01-trip-sync-list.png)
_The Trip Synchronizations list — past jobs with their scope, results and status, and the **New Sync Job** button._

## Step 2 — Create the job

Click **New Sync Job** and fill in:

- **Start Date / End Date** — the period to generate trips for. Use the **same day
  twice** for a single date.
- **Operator** _(optional — blank means all)_ — opens a **Select Operator** picker;
  search the table, then **Select** the row. Only **active** operators are listed.
- **Route** _(optional — blank means all)_ — leave as **All Routes** to include
  every route.
- **Day Filter** _(optional — nothing ticked means all)_ — **Weekdays** and
  **Weekends** tick their days in one click; **Clear All** resets. Ticking weekdays
  includes those days; **P.H.** _additionally_ includes every date that is a public
  holiday in any country.
- **Remarks** _(optional)_ — a note saying why you ran it. Worth filling in; it is
  what the next person sees in the list.

Then click **Create Sync Job**.

:::note[Date rules]
The **Start Date** cannot be in the past, and the **End Date** must be on or after
the Start Date.
:::

![New Sync Job form](/img/terminal-staff/generate-departure-trips/02-new-sync-job.png)
_The New Sync Job form — a date range, an optional operator/route scope, and the day filter._

## Step 3 — Read the preview

Creating the job opens its detail page, headed **Sync Job — _start_ → _end_**, with
its status badge and who created it. Three sections:

**1. Job Configuration** — the fields you just filled in, still editable while the
job is **Pending**. Correct anything with **Save Changes**, or scrap it with
**Delete Job**.

**2. Preview** — three counts: **To Create**, **To Delete** and **Conflicts**.
Below them, **Trips to Create** lists every trip the job will generate, grouped
**by date**, then **by route**, with each departure showing its **time** and
**operator**. Expand a date or a route to check the detail before you commit.
**Refresh Preview** (top right) re-runs the comparison if timeslots or trips have
changed since the job was created.

**3. Execute** — see the next step.

:::tip[What the three numbers mean]
- **To Create** — an active timeslot has no matching trip yet on that date.
- **To Delete** — a scheduled trip no longer matches any active timeslot **and has
  no check-ins or boarded passengers**, so it is safe to remove.
- **Conflicts** — an orphaned trip **already has passengers**. It is **skipped,
  never deleted**, and flagged for you to sort out by hand.

**0 to create / 0 to delete / 0 conflicts** means everything is already in sync.
Re-running a job is always safe — it is idempotent.
:::

## Step 4 — Execute

When the preview looks right, click **Execute Job**. A confirmation asks
_"Execute synchronization job?"_ and repeats that trips will be created and deleted
as previewed and that conflicts will be skipped. Click **Execute**.

The job is queued and processed in the background; its status moves **Pending →
Enqueued → Completed**. Reload the page to see the final counts.

![Sync job preview grouped by date and route, with the Execute Job button](/img/terminal-staff/generate-departure-trips/03-execute-job.png)
_The preview — **To Create / To Delete / Conflicts**, with the trip list expanded by date and route, and **Execute Job** below._

:::note[Only pending jobs can be changed]
**Edit**, **Delete** and **Execute** apply only while a job is **Pending**. Once it
is Enqueued or Completed the actions disappear — a completed job is a record of
what ran. To sync the same period again, create a **new** job.
:::

## Step 5 — Verify the trips

Open **Management → Departure Trips** and filter **Trip Date** to the period you
just synced. The generated trips appear with their code, operator, route,
departure time, status and passenger counts.

:::note[Type = Scheduled]
Every trip a sync job generates has **Type = Scheduled**. One-off trips you add by
hand are **AdHoc** — see
[Create an Ad Hoc Trip](/terminal-operator/create-ad-hoc-trip).
:::

![Departure Trips list for the synced date](/img/terminal-staff/generate-departure-trips/04-departure-trips.png)
_The generated trips for the synced period, each with **Type = Scheduled**._

## Running the same job twice

Re-running generation for a date you have already generated does **not** duplicate
anything. The job reconciles rather than re-inserts: a trip that already matches an
active timeslot is left exactly as it is.

## What a sync job does and does not touch

A sync job only ever affects **Scheduled** trips. **AdHoc** trips are never touched
by it.

### Which timeslots run on a date

The day rule is **additive** — a public holiday adds sailings and never removes the
weekday ones:

| The date is… | A timeslot runs when… |
| --- | --- |
| any date | its **weekday flag** for that day of the week is ticked |
| also a [public holiday](/terminal-operator/configure-public-holiday) in country X | _additionally_, its **Public Holiday** countries include **X** |

### How trips are reconciled

For each date in the range (subject to the **Day Filter**), the job looks at the
timeslots that run on that date — counting only **active timeslots whose operator
is also active** — and matches them against existing scheduled trips by **trip
code** (operator + route + date + departure + suffix):

| Result | When it happens | What execution does |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Created** | An active timeslot applies to that date but has **no matching trip** yet. | A new **Scheduled** trip is created from the timeslot, with its vessel and gate times. |
| **Deleted** | A scheduled trip **no longer matches any active timeslot** and has **no check-ins or boarded passengers**. | The orphaned, empty trip is **removed**. |
| **Conflicts** | An orphaned scheduled trip **has check-ins or boarded passengers**. | It is **kept and skipped**, and logged for manual review — move or clear the passengers, then re-run. |

:::info[What makes a trip "orphaned"]
A trip becomes orphaned when nothing generates it any more — its timeslot was
**deleted or deactivated**, its **operator was deactivated**, or a holiday-added
trip's **public holiday entry was deleted** (or the timeslot's holiday **countries**
no longer include it). Orphaned trips are then **deleted** if empty, or raised as a
**conflict** if they already carry passengers.
:::
