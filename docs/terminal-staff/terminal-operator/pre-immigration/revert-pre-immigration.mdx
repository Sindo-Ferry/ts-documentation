Sometimes a passenger is scanned and then has to step back out — wrong person,
forgotten luggage, a document problem spotted a second too late. **Revert To
Check-In** undoes the scan and puts them back to **CheckedIn**.

> **Pre-Immigration Scan → scan the pass → Revert To Check-In → Confirm Revert**

:::warning[Restricted access]
Reverting needs the **revert pre-immigration** permission, which is separate from
the scan permission. If you can scan but see no **Revert To Check-In** button, that
is why — ask your administrator.
:::

## When you can revert

| Condition | Required |
| --- | --- |
| The passenger's status | **PreImmigration** |
| The trip's status | **Scheduled** or **Boarding** |

If either is wrong, the revert is rejected — see
[If the revert is refused](#if-the-revert-is-refused) below.

## Step 1 — Bring the passenger up on screen

The **Revert To Check-In** button lives on the **green result card**, so you need
that passenger's card on screen.

- If you have **just scanned them**, the card is already there — go to Step 2.
- If the card has been replaced by a later scan, **scan their boarding pass again**.
  Re-scanning someone who is already PreImmigration is harmless — it returns their
  details without changing anything — and brings the card back with the button on
  it.

## Step 2 — Click Revert To Check-In

The red **Revert To Check-In** button sits at the bottom of the Passport Check card,
next to the note: _"Revert is optional. You can continue scanning the next passenger
without taking this action."_

## Step 3 — Confirm

A confirmation opens:

> **Revert Pre-Immigration Scan** — This will move **_[passenger name]_** back to
> CheckedIn status so they can exit through the gate if needed. They can be
> re-scanned at pre-immigration when they return.

Click **Confirm Revert** (or **Cancel** to back out).

## Outcome

- The passenger's status returns to **CheckedIn**.
- The trip's **Pre-Imm** count drops by one and its **Check In** count goes back up.
- The result card clears from your screen, ready for the next passenger.
- The passenger can be **scanned again later** when they come back. Nothing is lost
  and no new boarding pass is needed — it is the same pass, the same number.

## If the revert is refused

| Message | What it means | What to do |
| --- | --- | --- |
| _"Passenger must be PreImmigration to revert back to CheckedIn. Current status: Boarded."_ | They have already been scanned at the **boarding gate**, which moved them past pre-immigration. | Revert the **boarding** scan first — see [Revert Boarding](/terminal-operator/revert-boarding) — which returns them to PreImmigration. Then revert here. |
| _"Passenger must be PreImmigration to revert back to CheckedIn. Current status: CheckedIn."_ | Already reverted — someone got there first. | Nothing to do. |
| _"Trip must be in Scheduled or Boarding status to revert at pre-immigration gate. Current status: Closed."_ | The trip has been closed or departed. | Too late to revert through the scan page. Use [Update a Passenger's Status](/terminal-operator/update-passenger-status) if the record genuinely needs correcting. |

## Stepping a passenger all the way back

The two reverts chain. If someone was boarded by mistake and needs to come all the
way back to **CheckedIn**:

1. On [Boarding Scan](/terminal-operator/boarding-scan), scan their pass →
   **Revert To Pre-Immigration** → **Confirm Revert**. They are now
   **PreImmigration**.
2. Switch to **Pre-Immigration Scan**, scan the same pass → **Revert To Check-In**
   → **Confirm Revert**. They are now **CheckedIn**.

Each step needs its own permission, and each needs the trip still to be in a status
that allows it.

:::tip[Revert is a correction tool, not a routine one]
Every revert is recorded against the passenger. Use it to fix a genuine mistake —
not as a way to "reset" a passenger you are unsure about. When you are simply not
certain whether a scan registered, just **scan again**: repeat scans are ignored.
:::
