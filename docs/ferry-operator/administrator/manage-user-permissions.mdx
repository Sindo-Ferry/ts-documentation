A **direct permission** is granted to one person, on top of their roles. Use it
sparingly — it is the exception, not the tool.

> **Users → ⋯ → Manage Security → Direct Permissions tab → Grant / Revoke**

**Permission needed:** `operator.security.is_super_admin`

:::warning[Prefer roles almost every time]
Direct permissions are **invisible in the Roles list**. Six months on, nobody can
tell why one person can do something their colleagues cannot — and the only way to
find out is to open their profile.

Reach for a [role](/operator-administrator/add-role) first. Use a direct permission only for a
genuine one-off that no role should carry.
:::

:::warning[Only a Super Admin can change a user’s direct permissions]
The buttons in both panels are gated on **`operator.security.is_super_admin`** — not
on `operator.roles.write` or `operator.users.write`.

So an ordinary administrator can **open this page and read it**, but the action
buttons will not appear for them. If you can see the lists but not the buttons, that
is why.
:::

:::note
You need the permissions named above. The screenshots below use `https://ts-operator.sindoferry.com.sg/`.
:::

## Before you start

- You are signed in to the Operator Portal — see
  [Sign In to the Operator Portal](/ferry-operator/sign-in).
- The [user exists](/operator-administrator/add-user).
- You are a **Super Admin**.

## Step 1 — Open the Direct Permissions tab

Open **Users** → the user's **⋯** menu → **Manage Security**, then the
**Direct Permissions** tab.

| Panel | What it holds |
| --- | --- |
| **Available Direct Permissions** (left) | Permissions not currently granted to them directly. |
| **Directly Assigned** (right) | Permissions granted to them **individually**. Shown on a tinted background. |

Permissions appear as **raw strings** in monospace — `operator.passenger.cancel`,
`operator.scan.boarding`, and so on.

Both panels work the same way:

- **Filter box** — type to narrow the list. With hundreds of permission strings,
  this is the only practical way to find one.
- **Tick the rows** you want. The **checkbox in the header** ticks everything
  currently shown.
- The action button shows the count — **Grant (3)** — and stays **disabled
  until at least one row is ticked**.
- Each panel scrolls independently; the selection clears after every action.

## Step 2 — Grant

Filter, tick, then click **Grant (N)**. Effective immediately.

## Step 3 — Revoke

Tick rows in **Directly Assigned** and click **Revoke (N)**.

:::info[Revoking here does not touch their roles]
**Directly Assigned** lists **only** the permissions granted to this person
individually. If they also get the same permission from a role, revoking it here
changes nothing they can do — the role still grants it.

Check the **Effective Access** tab afterwards to see what they actually have.
:::

## How effective access is worked out

A user's effective permissions are the **union** of:

1. every permission from **every role** they hold, plus
2. every **direct permission** granted here.

There is no precedence and no subtraction — you **cannot** use a direct permission
to take something away that a role grants. To remove access that comes from a role,
either [revoke the role](/operator-administrator/assign-role#step-4--revoke-a-role) or
[change the role's permissions](/operator-administrator/manage-role-permissions).

The **Effective Access** tab shows the final combined list.

:::danger[You cannot subtract]
This is the single most misunderstood part of the permission model. If someone has
too much access, look at **where it comes from** — a role, a direct grant, or the
[Admin flag](/operator-administrator/grant-revoke-admin) — and remove it at the source. Nothing
here can override a grant.
:::

## When a direct permission is justified

| Situation | Better answer |
| --- | --- |
| One person covers an extra duty this week | **Direct permission** — genuinely temporary and personal. Remember to revoke it. |
| A whole shift needs the same extra access | Create or extend a [role](/operator-administrator/add-role). |
| One person always does something nobody else does | A role with one member is still clearer than a direct grant. |
| Debugging why access is missing | Fix the role. A direct permission hides the real problem. |

:::tip[Write down the ones you mean to remove]
Nothing expires and nothing reminds you. A "just for this week" grant survives until
somebody notices — keep your own note, and review the **Directly Assigned** panel
periodically.
:::

## If a change is rejected

| Message | What it means |
| --- | --- |
| _"Invalid permissions: …"_ | An unrecognised permission string was submitted. This should not happen from the UI; report it. |
