Look Up a Passenger

Someone comes to the counter with a question and no boarding pass. Passengers is where you find them. It searches every trip — yesterday's, today's, next month's — which is what makes it different from Current Trips, where you only ever see today.

Passengers → set the Trip Date range → search → View Passenger

This screen is read-only

The Passengers list is for finding and reading records. You cannot check in, edit, cancel, change status or print a boarding pass from here. The row ⋯ menu offers exactly one item: View Passenger.

To do something to a passenger, open their trip and work from its passenger table — see Acting on what you find.

catatan

You need permission to view passengers. The screenshots below use https://ts-terminal.sindoferry.com.sg/.

Before you start​

  • You are signed in to the Terminal Portal — see Sign In to the Terminal Portal.
  • Have something to search on — a passport / ID number, a name, or the trip and date the passenger travelled on.

Step 1 — Open Passengers​

In the sidebar, open Management → Passengers (route: /passengers).

Each row shows the passenger's Trip, No (passport / ID), Name (with gender beneath), Operator / Route, Type, Status and Nationality.

The Passengers list — a read-only view of passenger records across trips.

Step 2 — Set the date range you want​

The list opens scoped to today — this is the number one gotcha

When the page loads, Trip Date From and Trip Date To are both set to today. A passenger who travelled yesterday, or who is booked for next week, will not appear no matter how carefully you spell their name.

If a search comes back empty, check the trip-date chip first.

Click Show Filters and set the range to cover what you are looking for:

You are looking forSet Trip Date From / To to
Today's passengersLeave them as they are — both today
A past tripFrom = the travel date, To = the travel date
Anything within a periodFrom = start of the period, To = end of it
A future bookingFrom = today, To = a date past the trip
"I have no idea when"Widen both ends generously, then narrow with the other filters

There is no restriction to recent dates — the range can go as far back or forward as you need.

Step 3 — Search​

There are two ways to search and they behave differently. This catches people out constantly:

Quick search — partial match​

The search box at the top of the list does a "contains" match on No or Name. Type budi and you get everyone whose name contains "budi"; type A123 and you get every document number containing it.

This is the one to reach for by default.

Filter fields — exact match​

Inside Show Filters, the No (Passport No) and Name fields want the whole value:

The No and Name filters must match exactly

These two fields require an exact, whole-value match — the complete passport / ID number, or the passenger's full name. Matching ignores upper and lower case, but a partial value returns nothing at all.

Typing Budi into the filter's Name field when the record reads Budi Santoso gives zero results — and it looks exactly as though the passenger does not exist. Use the quick search box whenever you only have part of a value.

Narrowing further​

The Filter Passengers dialog also offers Operator, Route, Date of Birth, Passenger Type, Passenger Status and Nationality. Set what you need, then click Done.

The Filter Passengers dialog — Trip Date From / To at the top, the exact-match No and Name fields in the middle.

Every applied filter appears as a chip below the search box. Remove a chip to drop that one filter, or click Reset to clear them all. Refresh reloads the current results.

Read the chips before concluding "no such passenger"

The chips are the complete picture of what you are filtering on — trip date range included. When a search returns nothing, read the chips left to right. The answer is almost always sitting there.

Step 4 — Open the full record​

Open the row's ⋯ menu and choose View Passenger. A read-only details page opens with the whole record in three sections — considerably more than the list columns show:

SectionWhat it contains
Trips InformationsTrip Code, Trip Date / Departure, Trip Status, Trip Type (Scheduled or AdHoc), Operator / Route, Vessel, Boarding Gate / Berth
Passport InformationsNo, Name, Gender, Date of Birth, Issuance Date, Nationality, Issuance Country, Passport Expiry Date
Check In InformationsBoarding Pass No, Type, Status, Checked-in At, Pre-Immigration At, Boarded At, and whether they were Cancelled — with the time and who cancelled them

View Passenger — Trips, Passport and Check In Informations for one passenger on one trip.

The Check In Informations timestamps answer most counter questions on their own. They show how far the passenger actually got: a Boarded At time means they sailed; a Checked-in At with nothing after it means they never came through pre-immigration.

What you can answer from here​

Question at the counterWhere to look
"Did my colleague get on the boat yesterday?"Set the date to yesterday, search the name, read Boarded At
"What was my boarding pass number?"Boarding Pass No on the details page
"Which vessel and berth was it?"Trips Informations
"Was I cancelled, and by whom?"Check In Informations — the cancelled time and who did it
"Am I booked for next Tuesday?"Widen Trip Date To past that date, then search
"Is my passport expiry recorded correctly?"Passport Informations

Acting on what you find​

Once you have found the passenger, note their trip — then go to that trip to do anything:

  1. Today's trip: Operational → Current Trips → click the trip's row. An older or future trip: Management → Departure Trips → find the trip → open its detail page.
  2. Find the passenger in the table at the bottom of that page.
  3. Act from there:
    • Edit their passport details — CheckedIn only
    • Cancel them
    • Update their status
    • Reprint or download their boarding pass
Why the actions live inside the trip

Tying every action to a specific voyage protects data integrity. A passenger who travels weekly has one record per sailing; acting from a cross-trip list would make it far too easy to edit or cancel the wrong one. The trip's own passenger table leaves no doubt about which sailing you are changing.
