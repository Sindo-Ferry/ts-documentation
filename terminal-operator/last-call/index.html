Last Call

Last Call Recommended is an amber banner that appears on a trip's card on the Boarding Scan page. It is the system telling you: departure is close and there are still passengers waiting who have not boarded.

It is a prompt to make an announcement. It does not change the trip, it does not stop anything, and it does not need to be dismissed.

What it looks like​

An amber box at the top of the trip card, with a warning triangle:

⚠ Last Call Recommended 3 passenger(s) are still in pre-immigration and departure is in 12 minute(s).

If the trip has been marked delayed, the message says so too:

3 passenger(s) are still in pre-immigration and departure is in 12 minute(s) (delayed to 15:45).

A delayed trip with one passenger still in Pre-Immigration, eight minutes before its new departure time.

When it appears​

All four of these must be true at once:

ConditionDetail
The trip is BoardingNot Scheduled, not Closed, not Departed.
Passengers are still in Pre-ImmigrationThe trip's Pre-Imm count is above zero.
Departure is 20 minutes away or lessCounted to the effective departure.
Departure was no more than 30 minutes agoAfter that the banner stops showing — at that point you are well past a last call.

So the banner is live in a window running from 20 minutes before the effective departure to 30 minutes after it.

It counts pre-immigration only, not check-in

The number in the banner is the Pre-Imm count — passengers who have physically cleared pre-immigration and are inside the terminal waiting to board. They are the ones an announcement can actually reach.

Passengers still at Checked-In are not counted. They have a boarding pass but have not presented themselves at pre-immigration, so many of them are not in the building at all.

Delays are taken into account

The countdown uses the effective departure time: Delayed To when the trip is marked delayed, otherwise the scheduled departure. Record a delay with Update Timings and the banner recalculates itself — it will not nag you about a departure that has been pushed back.

The clock is the server's clock in the port's timezone, not your computer's. A workstation with the wrong time or timezone cannot make the alert fire early or late.

What to do when it appears​

  1. Read the number. Three passengers is an announcement; one passenger might be a quiet word with the gate.
  2. Make the last call over the terminal PA for that trip — code, operator, destination and berth are all on the card in front of you.
  3. Check who is actually missing. Click View PreImm Passengers on the trip card to see exactly who has cleared pre-immigration but not boarded — names and document numbers, so the announcement can be specific.
  4. Keep scanning. As the stragglers arrive and you scan them, the Pre-Imm count falls; when it reaches zero the banner disappears on its own.
  5. Decide. If passengers still have not appeared, close the trip. The close dialog warns you again, with a fresh count, before it commits.

Things worth knowing​

  • The banner appears and disappears by itself. The trip list refreshes every 10 seconds and after every scan, so the countdown stays current without you doing anything.
  • It is advisory. Nothing is blocked while it is showing, and nothing happens if you ignore it.
  • One banner per trip. With several trips boarding at once, each card carries its own banner and its own count.
  • It is not a record. Nothing is written down about whether a last call was made. If your terminal needs that recorded, note it in your own log.
