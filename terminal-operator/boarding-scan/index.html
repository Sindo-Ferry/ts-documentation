Scan a Boarding Pass at the Gate

The boarding gate is the last scan point. A passenger presents their boarding pass, you scan it, and the display tells them which berth to walk to.

Boarding Scan → scan the boarding pass → passenger is Boarded → "Proceed to Berth #…"

What the scan does: it moves the passenger to Boarded and raises that trip's Boarded count.

Restricted access

This page is only available to staff who hold the boarding scan permission.

note

The screenshots below use https://ts-terminal.sindoferry.com.sg/.

Before you start​

  • You are signed in to the Terminal Portal — see Sign In to the Terminal Portal.
  • You have set your active port. The Scan button stays disabled until you do, with the hint "Choose the port context from Settings before scanning."
  • The trip has been set to Boarding with a gate and a berth assigned.
  • The passenger display is running on the second screen — see Passenger Display Setup.

Step 1 — Open Boarding Scan​

In the sidebar, open Operational → Boarding Scan (route: /current-departure-trips/scan-boarding). The header shows your Active Port.

The Boarding Scan page — the scan field on the left, and every boarding trip at your port on the right.

One page covers every boarding trip

You do not pick a trip before scanning. There is a single scan field, and the system works out which trip each boarding pass belongs to and updates that trip's count. When three vessels are boarding at once, one officer at one screen handles all three.

Step 2 — Check the Boarding Trips panel​

The right-hand panel lists every trip at your active port that is currently Boarding or Closed. Each card shows:

  • The trip code and its status badge (Boarding / Closed).
  • Operator, Vessel, Departure Time, Gate / Berth and Destination Port.
  • Live counts — Check In, Pre-Imm and Boarded.
  • View PreImm Passengers (on a Boarding trip) — opens a searchable table of the passengers who have cleared pre-immigration and are waiting to board.
  • Set As Close (on a Boarding trip) or Set As Departed (on a Closed trip).
  • A Last Call Recommended banner when departure is close and passengers are still waiting — see Last Call.

The panel refreshes itself every 10 seconds while you are idle, and refreshes immediately after every scan. There is a manual refresh button too.

If it reads "No boarding or closed trips for now.", no trip at your port has been opened for boarding yet. Go and set the trip to Boarding first.

Step 3 — Scan​

Click into the Boarding Pass Number field, then either scan the barcode or type the 16-character number and press Enter. You can also click Scan.

After every scan the field clears itself and focus returns to it automatically, so you can work continuously without touching the mouse.

Step 4 — Read the result​

Accepted​

A green card appears with the passenger's document details — No, Name, Gender, Date Of Birth, Nationality, Issuance Country, Issue Date, Expiry Date — and the matching trip's Boarded count goes up in the Boarding Trips panel.

The passenger display turns green: Success — Proceed to Berth #[berth], with the operator's logo and the destination port.

A successful scan — passenger details, a Revert To Pre-Immigration button, and the trip's Boarded count updated.

The passenger-facing display — the berth number is the thing the passenger needs.

Rejected​

A red Invalid BP box appears on your screen with the reason, and the display turns red.

A rejected scan — the passenger is not boarded.

A passenger may board straight from Checked-In​

Skipping pre-immigration is allowed. A passenger whose status is CheckedIn can be scanned at the gate and goes straight to Boarded — no error, no warning. The trip's Check In count drops instead of its Pre-Imm count.

This is intentional: some terminals do not operate a pre-immigration desk on every sailing, and a passenger who slipped past it should not be stopped at the gate.

Rejection messages and what to do​

What you seeWhat it meansWhat to do
"Please enter boarding pass number."You clicked Scan with an empty field.Scan or type the number.
"Invalid boarding pass number."Not 16 characters, or no passenger has that number.Re-scan. If the barcode is damaged, find the passenger in View PreImm Passengers and read their BP number.
"Passenger boarding pass is not valid for the currently selected port."The pass belongs to a trip from a different origin port.Check your Active Port. This check runs before the passenger's status is looked at, so you will see it even for a passenger who is already Boarded.
"Trip must be in Boarding status to mark passenger as Boarded. Current status: Scheduled."The trip has not been opened for boarding yet.Set it to Boarding.
"Trip must be in Boarding status to mark passenger as Boarded. Current status: Closed." (or Departed)Boarding for that trip is over.They have missed it. Send them to the counter.
"Passenger must be CheckedIn or PreImmigration to move to Boarding. Current status: Cancelled."The booking was cancelled.Send them to the counter.
"Port context is required. Please select an active port in terminal portal."No active port is set.Set your port context.
What the passenger sees when a scan fails

Only "Invalid boarding pass number." is passed through to the public display. Everything else shows as "Please Check with our terminal staff". The real reason stays on your screen.

Scanning the same pass twice is safe​

Scan someone who is already Boarded and nothing happens — no error, and the count does not move. They are not double-counted. When in doubt, scan again.

Made a mistake?​

Use Revert Boarding — the Revert To Pre-Immigration button is on the result card.
